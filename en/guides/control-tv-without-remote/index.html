Can you control a TV without a remote?

Yes. You can use the TV’s physical buttons, your phone as a remote over Wi-Fi or, in many cases, the remote of another device connected to the TV by HDMI. The phone is the most complete option, with one condition: the TV must already be connected to Wi-Fi.

  • 3 min read
  • Updated Oct 12, 2026
  • App version 1.24.7

1. With the TV’s own buttons

Almost every TV has at least one physical button, usually under the screen, in the middle or behind one of the corners. On many models it is a single button that works as a joystick: press it to turn the TV on and open a menu, and move it to change volume, channel and input. It is enough to turn the TV on and do the basics, but slow for browsing apps.

2. With your phone, over Wi-Fi

If the TV is already on your home Wi-Fi, a remote app on the phone replaces almost everything the remote does: D-pad, volume, apps, keyboard and voice. The full step by step is in this guide.

The catch is the first pairing, which depends on the brand:

  • Android TV, Google TV and Fire TV: the TV shows a code on screen and you type it on the phone. No original remote needed.
  • Roku: there is no code. On newer Roku TVs, though, phone access comes set to Limited and has to be changed to Enabled in the TV menu.
  • LG and Samsung: the TV shows a request that must be accepted on the TV itself. Without the original remote, try accepting with the physical buttons, if your model lets you navigate with them.

3. With another device’s remote (HDMI-CEC)

Many TVs accept commands from devices connected by HDMI, such as a TV box, a set-top box or a game console. The feature has a different name on each brand (SimpLink, Anynet+, BRAVIA Sync and others) and usually lets you turn the TV on and off and change the volume with the other device’s remote.

4. A new remote

For everyday use, it is also worth buying an original or universal remote that matches your TV model. Until it arrives, the phone does the job.

Common problems

First, tap the green connection bar in the app and choose Test connection: in a few seconds it checks the Wi-Fi, the TV and the pairing, and tells you what to do.

My device does not show up in the search. What now?

Check that the device is on (not just standby) and that your phone is on the same Wi-Fi, not a guest network or mobile data. Some brands need phone control turned on in the device settings (see your brand under Compatible devices, in Settings). If it still fails, use Connect by IP. If nothing works, tap Report and tell me the device brand and model.

What is the code or PIN the device shows?

It is how the device confirms it is you pairing. It appears on the device screen and changes on every attempt, so look at the device right then and type exactly what you see. Android TV uses 6 letters or numbers, Fire TV, Panasonic and Philips use 4 numbers, and Kodi uses its web control password, and Sony uses a key (PSK) that you set yourself in the TV, under IP Control.

Frequently asked questions

Can I turn the TV on from my phone?

It depends on the model. On LG, the TV must be set up to power on over the network. On several brands the TV only turns off from the phone. To turn it on without a remote, the safest way is the TV’s physical button.

The TV is not on Wi-Fi. Can the phone help?

No. For the phone to control the TV over Wi-Fi, the TV must already be on the network. Use the physical buttons to reach the network settings, or get a new remote.

Is it really free?

Yes. The remote has no ads and no sign-up. In the future, some extra features may become part of an optional plan, but the basic remote stays free forever.
