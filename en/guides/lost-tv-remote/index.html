Lost your TV remote: what to do

If your TV is already connected to Wi-Fi, you can keep using it today with your phone as a remote. On Android TV, Google TV, Fire TV and Roku this works without the original remote; on LG and Samsung the first pairing must be accepted on the TV. For anything else, use the TV’s physical buttons until a new remote arrives.

  • 3 min read
  • Updated Oct 12, 2026
  • App version 1.24.7

What you need

  • The TV already connected to your home Wi-Fi (without it, the phone cannot talk to it).
  • An Android phone (Android 8.0 or later) on the same Wi-Fi.

Step by step

  1. Turn the TV on with the physical button

    Look for the button under the screen, in the middle or behind one of the corners. On many models the same button also opens a simple menu for volume and input.

  2. Install Comandoo on your phone

    Comandoo is a free app for Android 8.0 or later, with no ads. It is coming soon to Google Play; the link will appear on this site as soon as it is available. No account needed.

  3. Open the app and pick the TV

    With the phone on the same Wi-Fi as the TV, open Comandoo and tap your TV in the list. If it is not there, tap Find devices.

  4. Do the first pairing

    This is where the brand matters:

    • Android TV, Google TV and Fire TV: the TV shows a code; type it on the phone. Works without the original remote.
    • Roku: no code. If the TV does not respond, phone access may be set to Limited in the TV menu, and changing it means getting to Settings.
    • LG and Samsung: the request shows on the TV and must be accepted there. Try with the physical buttons, if your model lets you navigate with them. If not, you will need a remote for this first step.

    After the first pairing, the app reconnects on its own and you no longer need the original remote.

When to buy another remote

If the TV is not on Wi-Fi, or if pairing needs a request the physical buttons cannot reach, an original or universal remote that matches your model is the way. With it in hand just once, you connect the TV to Wi-Fi, pair the phone and use both from then on.

Common problems

First, tap the green connection bar in the app and choose Test connection: in a few seconds it checks the Wi-Fi, the TV and the pairing, and tells you what to do.

My device does not show up in the search. What now?

Check that the device is on (not just standby) and that your phone is on the same Wi-Fi, not a guest network or mobile data. Some brands need phone control turned on in the device settings (see your brand under Compatible devices, in Settings). If it still fails, use Connect by IP. If nothing works, tap Report and tell me the device brand and model.

What is the code or PIN the device shows?

It is how the device confirms it is you pairing. It appears on the device screen and changes on every attempt, so look at the device right then and type exactly what you see. Android TV uses 6 letters or numbers, Fire TV, Panasonic and Philips use 4 numbers, and Kodi uses its web control password, and Sony uses a key (PSK) that you set yourself in the TV, under IP Control.

Can I turn the device on from my phone?

It depends on the device. On LG, turn on Power on with mobile device (or Power on via Wi-Fi) in the TV and use Turn the device on over the network, after the app has connected once. On other brands, turning on over the network depends on the model and many can only turn off. Turning off from the app works on most.

Frequently asked questions

Can I connect the TV to Wi-Fi from my phone?

No. The phone only controls the TV once it is on the network. To connect the TV to Wi-Fi the first time, use the physical buttons or a remote.

What if my TV is another brand?

Many TVs run a system the app already covers: TCL, Philco, AOC, Multilaser and Semp are often Roku or Android TV. See the supported list.

Is it really free?

Yes. The remote has no ads and no sign-up. In the future, some extra features may become part of an optional plan, but the basic remote stays free forever.
